1 Overview

Where on the ocean surface do the animals and plants that we monitor as Essential Ocean Variables (EOVs) (Miloslavich et al. 2018) concentrate, and where do those concentrations fall on species that are at risk of extinction? This notebook answers that with a gridded product, aquamaps_eov, on the 0.05° global grid. It is both the pipeline that computes the product and the documentation of how it is made, so the method and the data cannot drift apart (Figure 1).

The framework in five moves:

  1. WoRMS AphiaID is the join key. The EOVs are defined, exactly as in obisindicators and obis-hex, by 33 seed taxa of the IOOS Marine Life Data Network (IOOS Marine Life Data Network, n.d.) that are expanded to all descendants in WoRMS (WoRMS Editorial Board 2026). Every taxon here carries the same AphiaID that the OBIS-based indicators use, so the two products are comparable taxon by taxon.
  2. AquaMaps suitability, not occurrences. For each species the relative environmental suitability (1-100) of AquaMaps (Kaschner et al. 2019; Ready et al. 2010), as delivered in MarineSensitivity release v9 on the 0.05° grid, is clipped to the IUCN range of the species where one exists, so that suitable but unoccupied habitat does not count.
  3. Share, the MarineSensitivity v10 way. A species’ distribution is turned into a share: the fraction of its area-weighted habitat that falls in each cell (MarineSensitivity 2026). Every species weighs the same in an EOV, however wide its range.
  4. Extinction-risk weights. Weighting each share by the equal-step IUCN Red List weights of Butchart et al. (2004, 2007) and the Ocean Health Index (Halpern et al. 2012) gives a risk metric beside the unweighted share.
  5. Petals and a composite. Per cell and EOV the shares are summed, converted to a density and scored 0-100. The EOV scores are the petals of a flower plot and their mean is the composite all. The result is published as a STAC collection that the OceanMetrics atlas reads, and that anyone can read with rstac, pystac or DuckDB.

AquaMaps models neither birds nor seagrasses, so the seabirds and seagrasses petals are absent by construction; the product has 5 populated EOVs out of 7, and the composite averages only the petals that exist in a cell.

3 Method

Notation: \(s\) is a WoRMS species (AphiaID), \(m\) an AquaMaps model of the species, \(c\) a 0.05° cell with area \(A_c\) (km²), and \(g\) an EOV.

1. Suitability. AquaMaps gives each model \(m\) a relative environmental suitability \(v_{m,c}\in[1,100]\) (Kaschner et al. 2019; Ready et al. 2010). A species with several models takes the cell maximum, the MarineSensitivity rule:

\[ v_{s,c} = \max_{m \in M_s} v_{m,c} \tag{1}\]

2. Range clip. Let \(R_s\) be the union of the cells of the IUCN range polygons of \(s\) (IUCN 2025). When \(R_s\) is not empty only suitable cells inside the range count; with no range the full AquaMaps distribution is used. A species whose suitable cells never meet its range has no habitat left and is excluded and counted.

\[ u_{s,c} = \begin{cases} v_{s,c}\,\mathbf{1}[c \in R_s] & R_s \neq \varnothing \\ v_{s,c} & R_s = \varnothing \end{cases} \tag{2}\]

3. Share. The share is the cell’s fraction of the species’ area-weighted habitat over its whole (global) distribution, so that \(\sum_c \text{share}_{s,c}=1\) (MarineSensitivity 2026):

\[ \text{share}_{s,c} = \frac{u_{s,c}\,A_c}{\sum_{c'} u_{s,c'}\,A_{c'}} \tag{3}\]

4. Weight. The Red List category of the species gives a weight \(w_s\) (Table 3): the equal-step weights of Butchart et al. (2004, 2007), adopted by the Ocean Health Index (Halpern et al. 2012). Data Deficient (DD), Not Evaluated and species without a category carry no weight and are left out of the weighted sum (they still count in share). The category comes from the Red List snapshot by accepted scientific name, and otherwise from the MST v9 taxon table.

\[ w_s = w(\text{category}_s),\qquad w_s \text{ undefined for DD, NE, none} \tag{4}\]

5. Per EOV. An EOV \(g\) is the union of the WoRMS subtrees of its seeds (Table 4). Per cell the two metrics are

\[ \text{share}_{c,g} = \sum_{s \in g} \text{share}_{s,c}, \qquad \text{risk}_{c,g} = \sum_{s \in g} w_s\,\text{share}_{s,c} \tag{5}\]

6. Score. Values are densities \(D = X / A_c\) (per km²), so that cell size does not drive the map. The score caps at the \(q\) quantile (default 0.99) of the densities over the cells where the EOV is present:

\[ \text{score}_{c,g} = 100\,\min\!\left(1,\ \frac{D_{c,g}}{q_{0.99}(D_g)}\right) \tag{6}\]

A cell is reportable for \(g\) when any taxon of \(g\) is present there; otherwise it is nodata. The risk score can be 0 in a reportable cell (no weighted taxon).

7. Composite. The equal-weight mean of the reportable EOV scores, so that the mean of the petals drawn in a flower plot equals the composite band:

\[ \text{score}_{c,\text{all}} = \frac{1}{|G_c|}\sum_{g \in G_c} \text{score}_{c,g}, \qquad G_c = \{g : \text{reportable}\} \tag{7}\]

The count \(|G_c|\) is stored as n_eov. An H3 twin at resolution 5 averages scores and densities with the cell area as weight. Two weighting schemes exist; ohi is the default and mst_v1 is the shifted variant of MarineSensitivity v1 (set with EOV_WEIGHTS).

4 Pipeline

The chunks below read the inputs, run the rules of R/eov_share.R in DuckDB, write the product to a staging directory and, only when asked, upload it (Figure 2). The long DuckDB pass over the AquaMaps rows runs in batches of species (EOV_BATCH_ROWS, default 80 million rows per batch); each batch writes small partial files (partials/totals and partials/cell_eov), which are the checkpoint: a re-render skips finished batches and EOV_REDO=1 recomputes them.

4.3 Distributions

The AquaMaps rows are read once, in batches of species (eov_make_batches()): species are ordered by EOV and AphiaID and filled up to EOV_BATCH_ROWS rows (AquaMaps plus range rows), a batch never splits a species and never mixes EOVs, and a species above the cap gets a batch of its own (Table 5). For each batch eov_batch_sql() builds the range footprint of the batch’s species, takes the maximum over a species’ models, clips to the footprint, and writes two small partial files: the species totals \(\sum_c u_{s,c}A_c\) and the per cell and EOV share and risk. A batch whose two partial files exist is skipped, which makes the run restartable (EOV_REDO=1 deletes the partials). eov_merge_sql() then sums the partials into eov_sp_total and eov_cell_eov. Memory and disk use are bounded by the batch size, not by the number of species or the size of the ranges.

# Overview {#sec-overview}

Where on the ocean surface do the animals and plants that we monitor as **Essential Ocean Variables
(EOVs)** [@miloslavichEssentialOceanVariables2018] concentrate, and where do those concentrations
fall on species that are at risk of extinction? This notebook answers that with a gridded product,
`aquamaps_eov`, on the 0.05° global grid. It is both the pipeline that computes the product and the
documentation of how it is made, so the method and the data cannot drift apart (@fig-framework).

![Framework: the WoRMS AphiaID joins the EOV seeds, the AquaMaps distributions, the IUCN ranges and the Red List; the share, weighted by extinction risk, is aggregated per EOV and scored; the STAC product feeds the atlas and other consumers.](diagrams/eov_framework.svg){#fig-framework .cc-diagram .nolightbox}

The framework in five moves:

1. **WoRMS AphiaID is the join key.** The EOVs are defined, exactly as in `obisindicators` and
   `obis-hex`, by `r n_seed` seed taxa of the IOOS Marine Life Data Network
   [@biddleMLDNEOVTaxonomy] that are expanded to all descendants in WoRMS
   [@wormsEditorialBoard2026]. Every taxon here carries the same AphiaID that the OBIS-based
   indicators use, so the two products are comparable taxon by taxon.
2. **AquaMaps suitability, not occurrences.** For each species the relative environmental
   suitability (1-100) of AquaMaps [@kaschnerAquaMapsPredictedRange2019;
   @readyPredictingDistributionsMarine2010], as delivered in MarineSensitivity release v9 on the
   0.05° grid, is **clipped to the IUCN range** of the species where one exists, so that suitable
   but unoccupied habitat does not count.
3. **Share, the MarineSensitivity v10 way.** A species' distribution is turned into a share: the
   fraction of its area-weighted habitat that falls in each cell [@marinesensitivity2026]. Every
   species weighs the same in an EOV, however wide its range.
4. **Extinction-risk weights.** Weighting each share by the equal-step IUCN Red List weights of
   Butchart et al. [-@butchartMeasuringGlobalTrends2004; -@butchartImprovementsRedList2007] and the
   Ocean Health Index [@halpernIndexAssessHealth2012] gives a `risk` metric beside the unweighted
   `share`.
5. **Petals and a composite.** Per cell and EOV the shares are summed, converted to a density and
   scored 0-100. The EOV scores are the petals of a flower plot and their mean is the composite
   `all`. The result is published as a STAC collection that the OceanMetrics atlas reads, and that
   anyone can read with `rstac`, `pystac` or DuckDB.

AquaMaps models neither birds nor seagrasses, so the **seabirds and seagrasses petals are absent
by construction**; the product has `r n_eov_all - length(eov_absent())` populated EOVs out of
`r n_eov_all`, and the composite averages only the petals that exist in a cell.

# Method {#sec-method}

Notation: $s$ is a WoRMS species (AphiaID), $m$ an AquaMaps model of the species, $c$ a 0.05° cell
with area $A_c$ (km²), and $g$ an EOV.

**1. Suitability.** AquaMaps gives each model $m$ a relative environmental suitability
$v_{m,c}\in[1,100]$ [@kaschnerAquaMapsPredictedRange2019; @readyPredictingDistributionsMarine2010].
A species with several models takes the cell maximum, the MarineSensitivity rule:

$$
v_{s,c} = \max_{m \in M_s} v_{m,c}
$$ {#eq-suit}

**2. Range clip.** Let $R_s$ be the union of the cells of the IUCN range polygons of $s$
[@iucn2025]. When $R_s$ is not empty only suitable cells inside the range count; with no range the
full AquaMaps distribution is used. A species whose suitable cells never meet its range has no
habitat left and is excluded and counted.

$$
u_{s,c} =
\begin{cases}
v_{s,c}\,\mathbf{1}[c \in R_s] & R_s \neq \varnothing \\
v_{s,c} & R_s = \varnothing
\end{cases}
$$ {#eq-clip}

**3. Share.** The share is the cell's fraction of the species' area-weighted habitat over its whole
(global) distribution, so that $\sum_c \text{share}_{s,c}=1$ [@marinesensitivity2026]:

$$
\text{share}_{s,c} = \frac{u_{s,c}\,A_c}{\sum_{c'} u_{s,c'}\,A_{c'}}
$$ {#eq-share}

**4. Weight.** The Red List category of the species gives a weight $w_s$ (@tbl-weights): the
equal-step weights of Butchart et al. [-@butchartMeasuringGlobalTrends2004; -@butchartImprovementsRedList2007],
adopted by the Ocean Health Index [@halpernIndexAssessHealth2012]. Data Deficient (DD), Not
Evaluated and species without a category carry no weight and are left out of the weighted sum
(they still count in `share`). The category comes from the Red List snapshot by accepted scientific
name, and otherwise from the MST v9 taxon table.

$$
w_s = w(\text{category}_s),\qquad w_s \text{ undefined for DD, NE, none}
$$ {#eq-weight}

**5. Per EOV.** An EOV $g$ is the union of the WoRMS subtrees of its seeds (@tbl-seeds). Per cell
the two metrics are

$$
\text{share}_{c,g} = \sum_{s \in g} \text{share}_{s,c},
\qquad
\text{risk}_{c,g} = \sum_{s \in g} w_s\,\text{share}_{s,c}
$$ {#eq-eov}

**6. Score.** Values are densities $D = X / A_c$ (per km²), so that cell size does not drive the map.
The score caps at the $q$ quantile (default 0.99) of the densities over the cells where the EOV is
present:

$$
\text{score}_{c,g} = 100\,\min\!\left(1,\ \frac{D_{c,g}}{q_{0.99}(D_g)}\right)
$$ {#eq-score}

A cell is *reportable* for $g$ when any taxon of $g$ is present there; otherwise it is nodata. The
risk score can be 0 in a reportable cell (no weighted taxon).

**7. Composite.** The equal-weight mean of the reportable EOV scores, so that the mean of the
petals drawn in a flower plot equals the composite band:

$$
\text{score}_{c,\text{all}} = \frac{1}{|G_c|}\sum_{g \in G_c} \text{score}_{c,g},
\qquad G_c = \{g : \text{reportable}\}
$$ {#eq-composite}

The count $|G_c|$ is stored as `n_eov`. An H3 twin at resolution 5 averages scores and densities
with the cell area as weight. Two weighting schemes exist; `ohi` is the default and `mst_v1` is the
shifted variant of MarineSensitivity v1 (set with `EOV_WEIGHTS`).

# Pipeline {#sec-pipeline}

The chunks below read the inputs, run the rules of `R/eov_share.R` in DuckDB, write the product to
a staging directory and, only when asked, upload it (@fig-pipeline). The long DuckDB pass over the
AquaMaps rows runs in batches of species (`EOV_BATCH_ROWS`, default 80 million rows per batch); each
batch writes small partial files (`partials/totals` and `partials/cell_eov`), which are the
checkpoint: a re-render skips finished batches and `EOV_REDO=1` recomputes them.

## Distributions {#sec-distributions}

The AquaMaps rows are read once, in batches of species (`eov_make_batches()`): species are ordered
by EOV and AphiaID and filled up to `EOV_BATCH_ROWS` rows (AquaMaps plus range rows), a batch never
splits a species and never mixes EOVs, and a species above the cap gets a batch of its own
(@tbl-batches). For each batch `eov_batch_sql()` builds the range footprint of the batch's species,
takes the maximum over a species' models, clips to the footprint, and writes two small partial files:
the species totals $\sum_c u_{s,c}A_c$ and the per cell and EOV `share` and `risk`. A batch whose two
partial files exist is skipped, which makes the run restartable (`EOV_REDO=1` deletes the partials).
`eov_merge_sql()` then sums the partials into `eov_sp_total` and `eov_cell_eov`. Memory and disk
use are bounded by the batch size, not by the number of species or the size of the ranges.

## Aggregate {#sec-aggregate}

```{r}
#| label: aggregate

# cells that have any value, with area ----
cells <- dbGetQuery(con, "
  SELECT cell_id, lon, lat, area_km2 FROM eov_cell
  WHERE cell_id IN (SELECT DISTINCT cell_id FROM eov_cell_eov)
  ORDER BY cell_id")
n_cell <- nrow(cells)
keys   <- eov_keys()
mk_mat <- \(x) matrix(x, n_cell, length(keys), dimnames = list(NULL, keys))

# densities per km2 (NA = EOV not reportable in the cell) ----
dens <- list(share = mk_mat(NA_real_), risk = mk_mat(NA_real_))
for (g in keys) {
  d <- dbGetQuery(con, glue("SELECT cell_id, share, risk FROM eov_cell_eov WHERE eov = '{g}'"))
  if (nrow(d) == 0) next
  i <- match(d$cell_id, cells$cell_id)
  dens$share[i, g] <- d$share / cells$area_km2[i]
  dens$risk[i, g]  <- d$risk  / cells$area_km2[i]
}
populated <- keys[colSums(!is.na(dens$share)) > 0]

# scores: q99 over the reportable cells of each EOV and metric ----
sc <- list(); q_rows <- list()
for (m in c("share", "risk")) {
  smat <- matrix(NA_integer_, n_cell, length(keys), dimnames = list(NULL, keys))
  for (g in populated) {
    present <- !is.na(dens$share[, g])
    s <- eov_score(dens[[m]][, g], q = q_score, ref = dens[[m]][present, g])
    smat[, g] <- s
    q_rows[[paste(g, m)]] <- tibble(eov = g, metric = m, q99 = attr(s, "q"))
  }
  sc[[m]] <- list(
    eov     = smat,
    all     = eov_composite(smat),
    n_eov   = eov_n(smat),
    dens_all = eov_density_all(dens[[m]]))
}
q_tab <- bind_rows(q_rows)

# band matrices in the published order ----
score_bands <- \(m) cbind(all = sc[[m]]$all, sc[[m]]$eov, n_eov = sc[[m]]$n_eov)
dens_bands  <- \(m) cbind(all = sc[[m]]$dens_all, dens[[m]])
stopifnot(identical(colnames(score_bands("risk")), eov_bands()),
          identical(colnames(dens_bands("risk")), eov_levels()))

n_report <- tibble(eov = populated,
                   n_cells = colSums(!is.na(dens$share[, populated, drop = F])))
```

## Verify against the published COGs {#sec-verify}

The inputs here are the arrays that the published AquaMaps COGs (`aquamaps_native`, 0.05° global)

# Reproducibility and use {#sec-use}

**Render.** The notebook needs the MarineSensitivity v9 files (`MSENS_DERIVED`, default
`~/_big/msens/derived`) and the CRAN packages loaded in the `init` chunk. A cheap test run on a
subset (first 50 species per EOV, written to `.../aquamaps_eov/test`, never uploaded) and the full
run are

```bash
EOV_TEST_N=50 quarto render aquamaps_eov.qmd          # test subset
quarto render aquamaps_eov.qmd                        # full run (about 12 GB of memory)
EOV_REDO=1 quarto render aquamaps_eov.qmd             # recompute the checkpoints
EOV_S3=1 quarto render aquamaps_eov.qmd               # also upload (the only side effect)
```

Flags: `EOV_REDO`, `EOV_S3`, `EOV_TEST_N`, `EOV_WEIGHTS` (`ohi` or `mst_v1`), `EOV_Q` (score
quantile), `EOV_BATCH_ROWS` (rows per batch), `EOV_VERIFY_N` (COG spot-check, 0 to skip), `MSENS_DERIVED`, `BUILD_MEMORY_GB`,
`BUILD_THREADS`, `BUILD_TMP_GB` (cap on DuckDB spill files, default 100). The rules live in `R/eov_share.R` and are guarded by `tests/testthat`
(`Rscript -e 'testthat::test_dir("tests/testthat")'`).

**In the atlas.** The OceanMetrics atlas lists the collection as its first child, so the default
layer is the first value of each `cube:dimensions` entry: `eov = all`, `metric = risk`. The
collection field `atlas:flower` (`dimension: eov`, `composite: all`) tells the app to draw a flower
plot on a cell click, with one petal per sibling item across `eov` for the current metric and the
`all` score in the centre. The atlas reads `atlas:bidx`, `atlas:rescale` and `atlas:colormap` on
the item's `cog` asset and the dimension labels in `atlas:labels`, so a new EOV or metric needs no
app change.
